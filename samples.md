## How to load them

**Option A — CSV import (fastest):**
1. Copy the CSV block at the end of this file into a file called `samples.csv`.
2. In the admin go to **Artefacts → Import CSV**, choose the file, check the preview, then import.
3. Open each artefact to upload its image, add the Hindi translation, and generate its QR code.

**Option B — by hand:** Admin → **Artefacts → New**, and copy the fields below.

**Images:** generate each image with the prompt given, save it as JPG/PNG, and upload it on the artefact's **Media** page (it is converted to WebP automatically). Paste the alt text into the image's alt-text field.

## 1. Chola Bronze of Nataraja

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Title | Chola Bronze of Nataraja |
| Category | Sculptures |
| Location | Sculpture Hall |
| Status | active |
| Period | c. 11th century CE, Chola dynasty |
| Material | Bronze (lost-wax casting) |
| Dimensions | 98 × 84 × 32 cm |
| Origin | Tamil Nadu, South India |
| Artist | Unknown Chola workshop |
| Discovered at | Temple hoard near Thanjavur, 1951 |

**Description**

Shiva as Nataraja, the Lord of Dance, performs the *ananda tandava* — the cosmic dance that destroys a tired universe so it can be created again. He dances inside a ring of flames that stands for the cycle of time, while his raised foot promises release from that cycle to anyone who seeks it.

Look closely at the four arms. The upper right hand holds a small drum whose beat starts creation; the upper left holds the fire that ends it. The lower right hand is raised in a gesture meaning "do not fear", and the lower left points to the lifted foot. Under his right foot lies a small figure representing ignorance, crushed but not destroyed.

Chola bronze casters used the lost-wax method: each figure was modelled in wax, coated in clay and fired, so every bronze is unique. Statues like this were carried through the streets during temple festivals — notice the holes in the base where poles were fitted.

## 2. Harappan Seal with Unicorn Motif

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Title | Harappan Seal with Unicorn Motif |
| Category | Antiquities |
| Location | Gallery 1 - Ancient Civilizations |
| Status | active |
| Period | c. 2500–1900 BCE, Indus Valley Civilisation |
| Material | Fired steatite (soapstone) |
| Dimensions | 3.2 × 3.1 × 0.9 cm |
| Origin | Mohenjo-daro, Sindh |
| Artist | Unknown Harappan seal-cutter |
| Discovered at | Mohenjo-daro, DK area, 1927 |
| Language | Indus script (undeciphered) |

**Description**

This tiny square seal is smaller than a matchbox, yet it carries one of the great unsolved puzzles of archaeology. Along the top runs a line of signs in the Indus script, a writing system that no one has yet been able to read.

Below the signs stands a one-horned animal, usually called a "unicorn", in front of an object that may be an incense burner or a ritual stand. The unicorn is the most common animal on Indus seals, and it may have been the emblem of a powerful family or trading group.

Seals like this were pressed into soft clay to mark goods and sealed bundles. Impressions of Indus seals have been found as far away as Mesopotamia, showing that merchants from the Indus valley traded across the Arabian Sea more than 4,000 years ago. The boss on the back was pierced so the seal could be worn on a cord.

**Hindi translation (hi)**

- **Title:** एकश्रृंगी आकृति वाली हड़प्पा मुहर
- **Description:** यह छोटी चौकोर मुहर माचिस की डिब्बी से भी छोटी है, फिर भी इसमें पुरातत्व की सबसे बड़ी अनसुलझी पहेलियों में से एक छिपी है। ऊपर सिंधु लिपि के चिह्न हैं, जिसे आज तक कोई पढ़ नहीं पाया है। नीचे एक सींग वाला पशु खड़ा है, जिसे "एकश्रृंगी" कहा जाता है। ऐसी मुहरें माल पर निशान लगाने के लिए गीली मिट्टी पर दबाई जाती थीं, और इनकी छापें मेसोपोटामिया तक मिली हैं।

## 3. Egyptian Heart Scarab

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Title | Egyptian Heart Scarab |
| Category | Antiquities |
| Location | Gallery 1 - Ancient Civilizations |
| Status | active |
| Period | c. 1300 BCE, New Kingdom, 19th Dynasty |
| Material | Green jasper with gold mount |
| Dimensions | 6.8 × 4.9 × 2.6 cm |
| Origin | Thebes, Egypt |
| Artist | Unknown Theban workshop |
| Discovered at | Tomb in the Theban necropolis, 1912 |
| Language | Ancient Egyptian hieroglyphs |

**Description**

Ancient Egyptians believed the heart, not the brain, held a person's thoughts and memories. After death the heart was weighed against the feather of Ma'at, goddess of truth. If it was heavier than the feather, the soul was devoured.

This scarab was placed over the heart of a mummy to help it pass that test. The flat underside is carved with a spell from the Book of the Dead, which begs the heart: "Do not stand up as a witness against me." In other words, it asks the heart not to reveal the owner's wrongdoings.

The scarab beetle itself was a symbol of rebirth, because Egyptians saw the beetle rolling its ball of dung as the god Khepri rolling the sun across the sky each morning. The polished green stone and gold band show this belonged to someone wealthy.

## 4. Portrait of a Florentine Merchant

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Title | Portrait of a Florentine Merchant |
| Category | Paintings |
| Location | Gallery 2 - Renaissance |
| Status | active |
| Period | c. 1490 CE, Italian Renaissance |
| Material | Tempera and oil on poplar panel |
| Dimensions | 61 × 44 cm (panel) |
| Origin | Florence, Italy |
| Artist | Workshop of a Florentine master (attributed) |
| Discovered at | Private collection, Siena; acquired 1964 |

**Description**

A wealthy merchant looks calmly out at us, dressed in a deep red cloak and a black cap. Behind him a window opens onto a hilly Tuscan landscape with a winding river — a detail borrowed from Flemish painters, whose work was greatly admired in Florence at the time.

Earlier portraits usually showed sitters in strict side profile, like a face on a coin. Here the merchant turns three-quarters towards the viewer, which feels far more alive and personal. This change was one of the big ideas of the Renaissance: a portrait should show a real individual, not just a status symbol.

Look at his hands resting on the ledge. He holds a folded letter, a hint at the trade and banking that made Florence rich. The painter has used thin glazes of oil over a tempera base, giving the skin its soft glow.

## 5. Marble Bust of a Roman Senator

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Title | Marble Bust of a Roman Senator |
| Category | Sculptures |
| Location | Sculpture Hall |
| Status | active |
| Period | c. 50 BCE, Late Roman Republic |
| Material | Carrara marble |
| Dimensions | 64 × 38 × 27 cm |
| Origin | Rome, Italy |
| Artist | Unknown Roman sculptor |
| Discovered at | Villa ruins near Tivoli, 1888 |

**Description**

Greek sculptors liked to show people as perfect and ageless. Roman sculptors of the Republic did the opposite. This senator has a lined forehead, sunken cheeks, deep creases around the mouth and slightly drooping ears — every sign of age is carefully recorded.

This style is called *verism*, from the Latin word for "true". For a Roman politician, wrinkles were something to be proud of: they showed experience, seriousness and years of service to the state. A youthful face would have looked less trustworthy.

Wealthy Roman families kept portraits of their ancestors in the entrance hall of their homes and carried them in funeral processions. A bust like this reminded visitors which family they were dealing with — and how long it had held power.

**Hindi translation (hi)**

- **Title:** रोमन सीनेटर की संगमरमर की आवक्ष प्रतिमा
- **Description:** यूनानी मूर्तिकार लोगों को आदर्श और चिरयुवा दिखाना पसंद करते थे, पर रोमन गणराज्य के मूर्तिकारों ने इसका उल्टा किया। इस सीनेटर के माथे की झुर्रियाँ, धँसे गाल और मुँह के पास की गहरी रेखाएँ ध्यान से उकेरी गई हैं। इस शैली को "वेरिज़्म" यानी "यथार्थवाद" कहते हैं। रोमन राजनेता के लिए झुर्रियाँ गर्व की बात थीं, क्योंकि वे अनुभव और राज्य की सेवा का प्रतीक थीं।

**Image alt text:** White marble bust of an elderly Roman man with a deeply wrinkled face and short hair.

**Image prompt**
